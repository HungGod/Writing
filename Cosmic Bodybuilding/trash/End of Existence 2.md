Inside what looked like a small, aging classroom, a peculiar demonstration was underway.

At the front stood a bodybuilder in faded blue overalls and a straw hat. Grinning toothlessly, he gave a deep bow to the audience.

Then, beginning his demonstration, his abs began to glow with a soft blue light. Reaching inside himself, he pulled out a golden dumbbell. Holding it firmly in front of him, he performed a standing bicep curl.

With each rep, his bicep pulsed brighter. From beneath his skin, a tiny sapling began to sprout. With every curl, the plant grew taller, until it reached full maturity. At its peak, a full husk of corn formed. Grinning with pride, the farmer plucked the cob from his arm, peeled it open, and took a loud, crunchy bite.

"Now who wants to make a career in Elpida being a farmer?" he asked, spreading his arms wide as if his words alone could lift the ceiling.

The students didn’t react.

Some yawned. A few stretched lazily, casting glances at the clock above the door. One coughed. Otherwise, silence.

The farmer’s grin faltered. "Hmph!" He spat out a few kernels, grumbling to himself in frustration he made his way back toward the other waiting presenters.

Next came two bodybuilders in crisp firefighter uniforms, stepping forward with exaggerated swagger. They launched into their own flashy performance, eager to win over the room with the thrill of their trade.

One flexed his muscles and promptly set himself ablaze by performing a series a strange muscular poses. The other struck a heroic double bicep, each arm flexing off to the side, as a firehose burst from his chest, dousing his partner in a dramatic spray of water.

Outside in the hallway, a blonde woman and a man in a brown suit watched through the narrow window with growing concern.

“Mrs. Polka, what is going on?” the man asked, his voice grave with anxiety. “Why aren’t the children interested in any of the careers offered here in Elpida? They are supposed to love our humble little town. Why are they giving such bored and vacant looks?"

His voice sharpened. 
"This is really bad! If we don’t find young replacements for our workforce, our town's services will fail, and we will be taken over by foreign forces." 
Lines formed on his forehead.
"The mayor gave us this task with the utmost urgency. You made a promise to him, that you would make it happen. Remember?”

Mrs. Polka flinched. The words of her superior caused her mind to kick into overdrive, scanning the eyes of her students’ disinterested faces she pushed up her glasses and adjusted her hair, as she tried to come up with a reason for her monumental failure.

“Dr. Gurny… I-I don’t know how this happened. I’ve always been diligent. I taught them the benefits of rural life, warned them of the dangers and violence of city living. I thought they understood…”

Her gaze continued to drift—until it landed on a particular boy. Her eyes narrowed.

Near the back of the classroom sat a boy with fluffy green hair and an ever-present warm smile. Unlike the others, his expression was one of intense focus. But his interest wasn’t in the performers’ magical feats—it was in finest details of their musculature and the shape and fullness of their physiques. An open notebook lay on his desk, where he was studiously taking notes on what he observed.

“Ged!” Mrs. Polka's eyes widened. A jolt ran through her like an electric shock. A seed of understanding beginning to sprout. “It’s him. Ever since he appeared… things have strayed from the plan.”

Dr. Gurny looked at her quizzically, then turned his gaze toward the boy, studying him in silence.

After a long moment, he shook his head.

“No. That boy is considered a hero. Everyone in town speaks glowingly of him. He’s constantly volunteering for charity work—he’s a pillar of our community,” he said quietly.  
“I refuse to believe his arrival to be the cause. There must be something else at play.”

Mrs. Polka’s expression tightened. “Perhaps that’s true… but he’s too dazzling. His figure casting too long a shadow. The other students have been driven to despair by his presence, their contributions feeling meaningless in comparison. Without meaning and purpose they have been driven away from feeling responsible for the future of this town."

"His charitable actions are greatly appreciated, yet I do wonder whether this kind personality of his is all an act. Every time there is mention for what he wishes to accomplish in the future, for which his response without fail is to end all of existence. The other teachers brush it off as some sort of childish joke, but I do wonder if he is speaking honestly with his words."

Dr. Gurny massaged his temple deep in thought. His expression settling on a deep frown.   
"It doesn't matter."
His voice cooled to a hush. “Elpida itself is an act. As long as he continues to display the selfless, righteous, can-do attitude for which Elpida is famous for, he can say whatever he wants. As long as the people still believe that the spirit of Elpida is still alive, whether or not the undercurrents themselves are rotten and evil does not matter.”

Mrs. Polka frowned, but seeing as Dr. Gurny ended the subject, she did not press any further.

Together they watched the concluding performances of the career demonstrations. 

One by one, local Elpida professionals performed their best stuff, showcasing their incredible abilities, holding nothing back.

But the children remained uninspired.

Aside from a flicker of interest during the Elpida police force’s dramatic takedown display of a mock-criminal, the students’ expressions remained detached.

Finally, the last performance ended. A pair of bodybuilders in construction helmets gave a polite bow, then walked off to the side.

The room fell into silence.

Mrs. Polka entered from the hallway, Dr. Gurny at her side. Clearing her throat, she addressed the class with stiff professionalism.

“That concludes our local Elpida career demonstrations. Let’s thank the brave men and women who shared their wisdom today. I hope all of you can show some appreciation for their efforts.”

Her words drew only light, half-hearted applause. Mrs. Polka’s jaw tightened, but before she could speak again, Dr. Gurny stepped forward.

"It seems our hometown heroes are not so popular." Dr. Gurny's voice echoed, his voice carried authority yet it was laced without a touch of enmity. "If not Elpida, I wonder what our senior class has planned for the future."  

The students ceased their clapping, being called out so blatantly, caused uncomfortable, awkward looks to appear on their faces.

Mrs. Polka's countenance returned soon after Dr. Gurny's call out. A smug smile on her face she pointed towards a girl with two pigtails near the front of the classroom.

"Your parents have owned a small grocer in our town for as long as I can remember. They are pillars for which our humble town relies upon, yet they aren't getting any younger. Bonnie, as their only child, I wonder what you plan to do when you graduate?"

Bonnie stiffened at Mrs. Polka's words, the meaning behind them were clearly provokational, yet still she stood up to confront his words directly.

"I wish to go to the city and study in higher education." Her voice spoke firm.

Mrs. Polka frowned, yet Dr. Gurny's expression remained unmoved.

"Higher education?" Dr. Gurny sighed. "A child of a grocer who dreams of pastures beyond the white picket fences of our humble town." Dr. Gurny shook his head, pityingly. "Such a shame, it seems one less grocer will be around in Elpida."

Bonnies face fell, and she drooped down into her seat, clearly Dr. Gurny's words struck a cord in her, no longer was she so set on her dreams of moving out to the city.

"I-I will not abandon the business entirely. If possible I will try and find someone else suitable to take it over. Even though I wish to leave, Elpida is still my home." Bonnie was quick to reply.

Dr. Gurny's face warmed up, a smile finally creeping onto his face. "Good, although its too bad that we do not have the opportunities to fulfill your great ambitions, I am glad to see you still hold our small home dearly within your heart."

Bonnie only felt worse hearing Dr. Gurny's words, slumping down in her seat, she grew listless, her ambitions wavering.

Mrs. Polka's face brightened further seeing the defeated face of Bonnie, pointing out a few more students, and Dr. Gurny without fail, pressured them into saying their wishes to preserve their hometown.

Pretty soon the room became sullen, as more students spoke their wishes to preserve the liveliness of their hometown, the ambitions of the students were dashed and some even felt pressured to take up jobs in Elpida, and began to praise and ask earnest questions of the career performers that stood patiently behind them.
