*Bang!*

A bolt of lightning split the sky, striking damp stone with a deafening crack. Rain lashed the hillside near the stronghold of Elpida, cloaking the earth in mist and shadow.

Kneeling at the summit, muscular cloaked figures were pulling on a purple, celestial rope, like a strange ritual of tug of war between man and the stars. An incredible storm formed in the clouds as if the acts of the men were defiant to the cosmos itself. 

Watching from a nearby ridge stood a broad-shouldered man, hardened by war and weather alike. Lord Ralph. He observed the ritual below with folded arms, his expression unreadable. Around him buzzed a swarm of advisors, ever eager to flatter, to validate, to bask in reflected power.

“Amazing,” Ralph murmured, eyes never leaving the storm. “For a tempest this fierce to strike during the birthing ceremony—surely, this is a sign. A batch of children is about to be pulled out from the most distant galaxies, children destined for legendary greatness.”

“Our town has long awaited fortune,” one advisor gushed. “The Celestial Mountain has finally heard our pleas!”

“Yes, yes, at long last!” another chimed in. “Lord Ralph is ever-wise! This is the dawn of a new age for Elpida!”

The flattery flowed freely. Ralph accepted it with a faint smile—until his expression shifted. His brow furrowed. His eyes settled on a man who stood apart.

“Enol…” Ralph’s voice turned rough and low, laced with suspicion. “You’re being too quiet. Do you doubt this sign? Are you wishing misfortune on our people?”

The other advisors fell silent. Tension crackled between them like the storm above. All had seen what happened to those who crossed Lord Ralph.

But Enol, calm as stone, showed no fear. He continued watching the storm, then slowly turned to meet Ralph’s gaze.

“I don’t question your judgment, my lord,” he said evenly. “But I do urge caution. Something about this year's storm feels… wrong.”

He gestured toward the sky. “The lightning roars, yet leaves no scorch marks. The rain falls endlessly, yet doesn’t pool. Look at our clothes—they’re soaked, but they still feel dry.”

Ralph frowned. He pressed down on his clothes, yet as Enol said it was not damp and moved freely not sticking to his skin. Then he extended his hand, letting rainwater gather in his palm.

At first, it sat cold and still.

Then—_drip_. It slid through his flesh like water through mesh, dripping onto the stone below.

His breath hitched. He flung his hand away with a shudder.

“Witchcraft! What is this demonic water?” one advisor stammered.

“Could it be the work of neuro-alchemists?” another gasped. “Have they cursed our minds with illusions?”

“An attack? A neuro-alchemist attack?!”

Panic swept through the group like wildfire.

“We must stop the ceremony at once!” a third advisor cried out. “The high priests must be protected!”

“Enough!” Lord Ralph’s voice thundered louder than the storm. “You think a handful of tribal neuro-alchemists could conjure something this vast? That they could fool _me_?”

Silence followed.

“We continue the ceremony,” he said, his voice cold and final. “This storm is no illusion. It must be be the work of Mount Existence, there is no other possibility. We will not squander this fortune.”

His glare swept over his advisors, each nodding quickly in fearful agreement. But again, his gaze found Enol—still calm, still distant.
“What about you, Enol?” Ralph asked, quieter now. “You agree with me, yes?”

Enol nodded slowly. “I do. We have already extensively scouted our surroundings, far as we know no one is nearby and even if they were I don’t believe any neuro-alchemist alive could conjure such a large illusion.”

He paused.

“But I wouldn’t rule out neuro-alchemy entirely. There are still forces beyond the stars who are capable of such feats.”

Ralph’s frown deepened. Anger flickered in his eyes. But after a moment, he let out a long breath and turned back to the ceremony, saying nothing more.

Another bolt descended, crossing over a tendril of lightning. Each bend of light sparked and shifted in color. The once-yellow strand turned a glowing shade of orange.

“Fate crossing!” an advisor gasped.

“What an auspicious omen! Orange lightning—imagine the blessing!” another exclaimed.

But their awe was cut short. A second bolt fell, folding the orange light into red. Nearby tendrils were drawn toward it, pulled into the growing strand like moths to flame.

Ralph laughed—loud and manic. His eyes gleamed with wonder.

But his laughter faded into stunned silence.

A third bolt fell. Then a fourth. More and more rained down, folding the lightning again and again. Colors merged, deepened, brightened—until all that remained was a brilliant, ethereal white bolt that shone like the midday sun.

For a moment, the storm’s center parted—and there, suspended in the clouds, was the apparition of a mountain, its peak hidden in the storm, its radiant form even brighter than the bolt that had birthed it.

Ralph’s smile vanished. His face turned solemn.

“An apparition of Mount Existence...” he whispered. “Even the heavens recognize his talent, humbling themselves before it with holy light.”

He turned to his men. “Study this vision carefully. Within its slopes lie secrets—insights into the fabric of life itself. If one is destined, even strength can be awakened from this sign.”

The advisors were speechless. Eyes wide, hearts pounding, they stared at the mountain in reverent silence.

Ralph stayed fixed on the white bolt.

“A child... destined for an unthinkable future. One who has inherited the fate of his unborn brothers and sisters. His footprints shall thunder through all the land.”

He raised a fist to the heavens.

“The transcendent warrior. My son and inheritor of Elpida... Ral!” he roared.

Cheers erupted. The advisors erupted in praise, congratulating their lord for bestowing such a powerful name.

But just as the excitement began to settle, one voice broke through the chorus.

“And what of the second child?” Enol asked quietly.

“Second child?”

“What nonsense are you spewing, Enol?” an advisor snapped. “I see only one strand of fate. Two children can’t share a birth. That’s impossible.”

The others looked to one another, unsure.

Ralph’s eyes narrowed. He turned back toward the light.

It was faint—barely perceptible in the mountain’s glow—but there, hidden within the halo of the white bolt, was a shadow. A second strand. So small and dim, it could easily have been missed.

“That child,” Ralph said slowly, “with a fate born in the shadow of such brilliance... I can’t imagine he’ll do anything of importance.”

His voice carried both pity and finality.

Enol stepped forward.

“A child grown in the shadow of his awe-inspiring brother. Perhaps it is his misfortune... or perhaps it’s his resilience. Like a flower blooming in stone, we should not be so quick to dismiss him.”

Ralph was stunned for a moment—then let out a booming laugh.

“Good!” he bellowed. “Well said, Enol. I was too quick to judge. But what shall we do with such a tenacious soul?”

He looked at Enol, a glint of mischief in his eye.

“Would you raise him, Enol? This tenacious child?”

The other advisors turned with daggered stares. Jealousy smoldered in their eyes.

But Enol didn’t flinch.

“If it is your will, my lord,” he said with a graceful bow, “then I will do my best. Brother of Ral the Magnificent shall be called... Ged the Tenacious.”

Silence followed.

Ralph finally spoke, his voice soft but resolute.

“Ged. A fine name. And with your guidance, Enol, I trust he will become a great asset to Elpida.”
