The congregation knelt in silence, the air thick with candle smoke, Ged watched on from the entrance.

“Preceptor Gore,” one of the bowing men whispered, his voice trembling as though it might shatter. “I am but a climber lost upon the slopes of Mount Existence. I beg your guidance.”

“Speak, and I will listen.” Gore’s tone was soft, yet it carried like thunder, filling every ear in the chamber.

The man’s lips quivered. “Last night I strayed… I sought the Peak of Existence.” He broke off, chest hitching, eyes wide with terror. “And this morning I woke up with a demon inside my mind.”

Gore stroked his beard with one hand. "What has caused you to veer away?"

"My growth has stumbled. My workouts once carried me to the first-level, but now I’ve plateaued. And as my body stalled… so too did my job, my marriage, my will to keep climbing. I-I fear there’s nothing left for me in this life. My climb has ended."

Gore closed his eyes as he listened. When the man finished, he opened them to speak.

"You feel as if you are not growing, but look around you the days still pass, your climb has not stopped. Your growth has stagnated, and stagnation is painful, but this is all transitory, it will pass with time."

"I think I understand Preceptor..." The man trembled with indignation. "But... life--life has gotten so hard, every day has been worse than the last. The demon inside me grows even as we speak. I can't help myself but dwell on thoughts of the end."

Gore's eyes gleamed with ethereal light.

"To climb the Mountain is to suffer. In suffering, it is easy to lose the path. Easy to think only of reaching the end."

"But the end of one, is the end of all. The Peak of Existence, is the end of all existence." 

"The Peak cannot be reached. The climb can never end."

"The demons are jealous of our existence and wish to seize our minds and bodies. Starve them of your thoughts, don't let them succeed in possession."

The man bowed deeply. "I see... Thank you for your guidance, Preceptor." 

“Today I grant you relief. Tomorrow is yours to endure,” Gore intoned, his eyes burning like flames. “Go, sit before me, and receive this blessing.”

The man rose from his bow and sat before him.

Gore clasped his hands in prayer. Neural essence crackled between his fingers like lightning, his body radiant with white light. Muscle energy pulsed in waves, rippling the air around him.

He spread his hands apart. Sparks leapt back and forth, weaving threads of light, until they converged into the outline of a worm.

A ring of recognition burst out, the worm solidifying into a corporeal existence, visible to all.

Ged felt the ring pass through him, his worries reduce by a tinge.

_Thought Burrowing Worm… no matter how many times I see Father perform his Neuromanifestation techniques, it is still an impressive sight to behold._

The worm writhed between Gore’s fingers. He brought it down and laid it gently at the man’s ear. It perked up, as if sensing purpose, then burrowed eagerly into his skull.

The man’s face slackened with relief. Wordlessly, he rose, gave one final bow, and walked toward the exit where Ged was.

Ged watched him go, his vacant eyes brushing shoulders. Whatever the man experienced when taking the worm, was information he was not allowed to understand.

Gore's gaze locked in on Ged for the first time since he had entered.

"I have recognized your arrival. What is it you need child?" Gore spoke, his tone neither warm nor cold.

“Father,” Ged spoke measured, “I have come to seek your guidance on a personal matter. I hope you will find the time for me.”

Gore frowned, stroking his beard. He was silent for a long moment.

“Very well. Wait in the back chamber. I will see you when I have finished with the others.” His eyes slid away, already fixed on the next patron who stepped forward to bow.

A dull weight pressed in Ged’s chest. Dissatisfaction flickered, though he had long grown used to such treatment. Without a word, he skirted the central platform and made his way toward the curtained side chamber.

He stepped inside. White polished skulls lined the walls like library shelves. A workbench crowded with beakers and vials held fluids and stones. At the room’s center was a block of white marble, the Mind Magnification Stone, its surface alive with faint fluctuations of neural activity.

Ged lowered himself onto the mat and closed his eyes.

The stone vibrated with a low hum. Warmth spread through it and seeped into his skull.

His focus turned inward. His consciousness appeared as a swamp of strings. Each one was a connection: the feel of grass beneath his hand, the smell of wet stone, the sight of living flesh. They squirmed and multiplied without end. Ged trudged through the red mire as he neared his mind’s center.

There, suspended, hovered a pin-sized black hole. Orbiting it was a fragment of blue sky.

Ged squinted, testing it, even trying to summon anger. The black hole did not stir. 

“What is it? Is it asleep?” Suspicion gnawed at him. "This hole is just to vast to get a good read, I can't peer further than a hair."

He shook off the bad premonition, turning his gaze to the sky fragment. As it circled the pin-sized hole he saw faint wobbling, its path uneven.

“This piece of sky’s path is not uniform,” he muttered. “It is drifting inward, being pulled into the Mind Shadow?”

The fragment left behind a faint comet trail, spiraling inward. At one point in orbit it would jolt, and resist its pull, although it didn't fully correct its course.

Ged raised an eyebrow, leaning closer. 

The piece of sky, was the same as usual, blurry galaxies rested within spinning gently, illuminated with countless stars. But as he watched it pass through the jolt in its orbit, a flash of blue light emitted from a galaxy cluster.

Ged watched it closely, each orbit he studied the galaxy cluster. It was faint, but whenever the jolt happened, the taste of salt permeated his mouth. 

"Ocean salt?"

Ged widened his view of the cluster, each galaxy glowed with a light, and once his view was far enough away a blurry image began to form.

"Is that Bowl?" Ged studied the image, where he saw the vagueness of enclosed stone walls, concealing a black pit inside. "But why? Is it because at Bowl's gate I experienced the ocean vision? Are these things leftover residue from that?"

Ged held his chin in thought, but after a moment's thought, he couldn't come up with anything conclusive.

Exiting his consciousness, he opened his eyes on Mental Magnification Stone inside the backroom.

Thoughts still lingering about the new objects that appeared inside his mind he turned his attention elsewhere, reaching into his abs he brought out a clay tablet that was his expired permit.

_I didn't lie to the officer, that man was wrong this permit was truly obtained through legit means, and I truly am on my way to renew it._ 

Ged scanned the details, it detailed a special extraordinary illness permit, a rare and odd permit, given to those inflicted by a Mind Shadow, or of similar ordinance. 

But as he continued to read a wry expression formed on his face, as the bottom detailed a five stone renewal. _I am currently stone-less. Too poor to renew it._

His thoughts lingered on the Abyssal Mouth. His original reason for coming was to obtain stones. Yet he ended up taking such a long detour. A helpless expression on his face. 

_Perhaps I was too hasty to seek the advice of Father, I could done a trip to the Mouth first. I was overeager to examine my situation, I should have secured some stones before coming._

His thoughts dragged on his father's slow moving ways and couldn't help but sigh inwardly. He took out the empty vial of Mind Soothing pills and the fractured thug's skull and looked at each thoughtfully.

_It was a steep cost, and even though the results are not clear, they seem promising._ He couldn't help but smile as he gazed upon the empty vial. _At the very least I won't need pills for the coming days._

He put away the empty vial back in his abs, and held the fractured thug skull carefully with his hands, not wanting to break it further.

_I wonder what the origins of the mental treasure were. I wonder... can it be created a second time?_

Ged’s fingers traced the fractured skull, just about to utilize his mind reading technique, when the curtain stirred. He froze.

"Father..." Ged watched as Gore's figure emerged from the curtain, his face calm and unreadable.

"You are not going to believe what had happened... my Mind Shadow. It has reduced!" Ged spoke joyously, his words did little to move the wooden expression of his Father.

Gore walked noiselessly, he didn't answer Ged, not even a nod. He inched closer.

"I feel free. Like an entire mountain was lifted off my back. For once in my life, I feel as if I have hope to experience a recovery."

Gore’s face didn’t move. His wooden expression carved the joy from Ged’s words.

Then his brow furrowed. “Be silent. Let me see for myself.”

His fingers emitted intense fluctuations of muscle energy. Sprawling them across Ged's scalp, he performed the mind reading technique. 

Although this technique was far more domineering than Ged's, not even needing the use of a Soul Taser, his fingers blurred with precise motions, combing over every inch of Ged's scalp.

It started as an itchy sensation a feeling as if his scalp needed a scratch, then it increased in intensity, until Ged couldn't help but grit his teeth, the pain hard to bear.

A few breaths later it ended. Gore withdrew, leaving Ged slumped and gasping for air.

“Hmm…” Gore stroked his beard, eyes closed, furrows etching deep into his brow.

Ged had finally propped himself up enough, to look Gore in the eye, clouds of anger forming within. _Could you not have listened to my words? Am I not trustworthy enough for you?_ Ged felt pain well up in his heart, yet he didn't voice it. He was still his father, and he needed his help to learn about the Mind Shadow, so he endured the feeling, bottling it up deep inside.

Gore finally opened his eyes, his eyes slanted.

“I don’t like it.” His tone was cold, final. “That ocean treasure—at first glance it looks as though it weakened your Mind Shadow. But what it truly did was hollow your mind, carving out more space for it to expand. This is no cure. It is a garden. Your illness now has ideal conditions to grow unchecked. It sleeps only because it feeds. When it wakes, it will be stronger than ever."

Ged's face fell at the words, then his hands clenched into fists. "Father… could you be mistaken? Yes, the shadow may grow—but so will I. The hollowing out of my mind may have been great benefit to the Shadow, but it was also beneficial to I. So what if it grows stronger? As long as I can fight it, I will find a way to win. Isn't that what you wanted me to do in the first place?"

Gore took a step back, at this point, he was stroking his beard even more than normal. His ethereal gaze locked in on Ged thoughtfully. 

“Fair enough,” Gore said at last. “There are mysteries here. Brooding over them will not help. Give me the skull. I will examine the treasure’s origin. I suspect demonic interference.”  

Ged hesitated, fingers tight on bone.  

Gore’s tone softened. “Ged, my child, I know you want to uncover the truth yourself. This matter is grave, it concerns people far beyond yourself. If you entrust the skull to me, I will grant you one request. As long as it is within my power I will fulfill it.”  

“Teach me the Thought Burrowing Worm technique,” Ged said resolutely, meeting his father’s eyes. "Then I will let you have the skull."
