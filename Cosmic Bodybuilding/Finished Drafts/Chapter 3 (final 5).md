The ground fissured outside the city gate, revealing a lava tunnel. Inside, two engines revved, growing louder as they neared the surface.

Two massive lizard motorcycles shot out of the tunnel, wailing like sirens. Their headlamps flashed a crimson handprint insignia that seemed to shine through stone. Two bodybuilders in police uniforms rode them.

The lizard motorcycles rolled toward the thug corpses and the discarded worm.

The leader chewed on a cigar, eyes hidden behind dark-rimmed sunglasses, his dark hair slicked back. He was incomparably massive. If his chest were a room, it could fit a desk and a chair.

His partner was smaller, but his scarred face and sword-slanted brows lent him a menace no bulk could match.

“Lieutenant Wattkinz, take a look…” The vicious-looking officer swung off his lizard motorcycle and paced the scene. “Looks like a robbery gone wrong. But look how ruthless our guy is. He left all the victims’ belongings, destroyed one head, and took the other.”

“That’s one serious grudge. Perhaps some fed-up vigilante is to blame.” He glanced at the thin thug’s corpse and shook his head.

“You were riding up front. Spot anyone suspicious?”

Wattkinz didn’t dismount. He held back his answer and chewed his cigar, staring at Bowl’s fractured entrance with a mysterious grin. “How many nutritional stones were left behind?”

His partner grinned and crouched, cutting open the corpses’ abdomens to search inside. He counted the bloody nutritional stones in his palm. "Not bad. Eleven and a half. Low-grade."

Wattkinz exhaled a raspy chuckle. “That’s that, then. No leads, no suspects. Case is cold. Bag the evidence.”

“Yes, sir.” The scarred man called his lizard motorcycle over. It unhinged its jaw and swallowed the corpses whole, then slurped up the discarded worm like a noodle.

Wiping his hands, the scarred officer remounted. He glanced at Wattkinz. “You’ve been quiet today, boss. What’s on your mind?”

Wattkinz sat unmoved, ash drifting from his cigar.

“Nothing much. A moment ago I saw my brother at the entrance.”

His yellow-stained teeth gleamed coldly. “I haven’t seen him in years. You go on ahead. I’m going to pay him a quick visit.”

Ged felt a shiver down his spine, as he hurried along cracked sidewalks, his figure blending into the crowd.

Although he was technically inside Bowl, this was still the outskirts. Constant meteor barrages kept the surface harsh and underbuilt. Because of its proximity to the city’s core, sprawling slums took root here; residents were down on their luck, with little food or hope.

Untrained civilians. Physiques gaunt and lifeless. Some begged in the streets, others sold odds and ends. After the Celestial Offering, many had their homes destroyed, and most were moving rubble to build makeshift shelters.

Ged paid little attention. It was the usual sight. He kept moving until he neared the underground entrance to the city’s core.

Two lines snaked toward two kiosks, where burly bodybuilder officers screened people before they entered.

One was the express lane, the other the civilian entrance.

At the front of the lines, officers with metal batons swept them over each person’s muscles, checking density and mass. The express lane collected a fee. The civilian lane was free.

The lines were mostly split by physique. First-level Muscle Condensation and below waited in the civilian lane, while those at second level and above paid for the express lane.

Ged took one glance at the civilian lane. It snaked for kilometers into the distance, and the officers at the front were screening as slowly as possible. If he queued there, he wouldn’t get in until evening. Without hesitation, he headed for the express lane.

The express line moved quickly.

The officer swept his baton over a young bodybuilder and raised an eyebrow. As the pass moved over his quads, the scanner pinged above the civilian limit.

“Your quads exceed civilian limits. Do you have a permit?”

The young bodybuilder grew nervous. “Officer… I’m sorry, I—I didn’t realize. My last leg workout, I must have gotten carried away.”

The officer’s face stayed blank. “Carried away, huh?” He glanced at the readout. “Since this is a first offense, I'll let you off with a warning. Be careful with your leg workouts going forward.”

The young bodybuilder thanked him profusely, paid the one-stone fee, apologized again, and went inside.

Ged frowned. The officers were different today. Civilians got barked at and shoved along; lifters got patience and easy passages. The favoritism wasn’t new, yet it was far more brazen than before.

At the express counter, an untrained man stepped up. The officer had been chatting easily with a second-level bodybuilder. When the newcomer approached, the conversation cut off.

The officer slapped his baton into his palm and fixed him with a hard stare.

“What are you doing here, beggar? You’re in the wrong line.” The officer’s words beat down on the man like the hot sun, causing sweat to form on his brow.

“Uh, good day, officer. I’m in a hurry to get inside the city. This is the express lane, right? Don’t worry, I have the stones to pay.” He tried to sound confident, but inside he was shaken.

The officer snorted and spat. He swept the baton over the man, scanning his muscle density. “Level Zero. Two nutritional stones to enter.”

“Two? But the other guy paid one.”

“New rules. Able-bodied citizens pay one. You lazy, soft-bodied coasters pay double.”

The man’s face fell. He reached into his bag and begrudgingly produced two stones. The officer turned them over in his hand, his frown deepening.

“I don’t think these are real. Wait here while I get them appraised.”

Without warning, metal bindings shot from the kiosk and clamped the man’s wrists and ankles, trussing him tight. He jerked against them, rage bright in his eyes. “What is the meaning of this? You—”

A metal gag snapped over his mouth, and he fell silent.

The officer hauled him from the line and dumped him on the tiles. “Next.”

Many of the untrained civilians in the entry line saw the scene and felt anger surge.

Roars of protest rang out.

“Bastards! Who are you trying to protect?”

“Can you be any more corrupt?”

On the floor, the man rocked back and forth, eyes bloodshot, his teeth clenched so hard against the gag that his gums began to bleed.

A small protest broke out as people stepped from the line to shout at the officers. More police poured in, batons popping, and the queue fell back into place.

Ged couldn’t help but frown. The treatment of the untrained felt excessive, yet he couldn’t bring himself to stand up for them. Because of his Mind Shadow, he had to fight for every inch or die; for that, he kept little sympathy for the untrained, who seemed too lazy to help themselves or look toward a better life.

The line moved again. Not a single bodybuilder stood up for the untrained. Like Ged, they kept their eyes forward, and the queue crept on.

Through an almost Herculean effort, the untrained man chewed through his gag. His mouth was bloody, several teeth gone. He glared at the bodybuilders who hadn’t so much as lifted a finger.

“Cowards. Not a spine among you. Can none of you stand up for anyone but yourselves? Will you all watch, vacant-eyed, while your fellow countrymen are beaten and bruised?”

The bound and gagged rocked in their restraints, yet no one dared answer.

Ged reached the front of the line and stepped up to the officer.

The officer waved his baton across Ged’s physique. The scanner pinged, and he raised an eyebrow.

“Third-level Muscle Condensation,” he muttered, expressionless, hand outstretched. “Permit, please.”

Ged reached into his abs, retrieved a clay tablet, and handed it over.

The officer poured muscle energy inside and read. “It’s expired.”

Ged took out two nutritional stones and handed them to the guard. “I’m on my way to renew it.”

The officer hesitated, chuckled, then snatched the stones from his palm. “Make sure you take care of this as soon as you get inside.”

The man’s face went red. “Seriously? Officer, that permit is clearly expired. You can’t take those stones and let him off while I’m treated like trash.”

Ged met his gaze.

“What are you looking at? Have you finally taken pity on me?” the man said, glare sharpening.

“What pity? If you had more strength, you wouldn’t be in this situation. You got what you deserve,” Ged said curtly.

“You think strength would fix me?” he spat. “Even if I crawled to level two—what then? There are no jobs. No future. Just another dead end. So what am I supposed to do? Bribe? Bleed for every scrap? Just to say that I tried? Tell me—” his voice broke into a jagged laugh—“what paradise am I supposed to be reaching here?"

Ged balled his fist, ready to answer, when a second gag shot from the kiosk and clamped over the man’s mouth. His eyes narrowed as he glared at the officer with renewed hatred.

The officer flicked a glance at Ged. “Move along.”

Swallowing his retort, Ged walked through the tunnel toward the city center.

He tried to shake off the man’s words, but they lingered, gnawing at his very core.  
_This man rejects strength. But what other path is there? Maybe there is no paradise, maybe it’s all a lie. But to rot? To be prey? To wait for rescue without trying to rescue yourself? That I can never accept._ His fist clenched. 

Agitation rose, then faltered. 

A flicker of surprise struck him as his chest lightened.  
_Wait. No reaction? The Mind Shadow didn’t stir!_

Curiosity creased his face. _The changes in my condition are still unclear. Along with the sky ocean fragment I seized, I need to probe them more thoroughly to find out what changes they have brought me._

_Even that may not be enough. This situation is too bizarre… I should seek a second opinion._

Only one person came to mind. _Father…_ His expression twisted with conflict.

Ged grew up an orphan, abandoned in a distant province and taken in by the Bowl branch of the Temple of the Peakless Mountain. He had many good memories of the place, but his feelings about his father were mixed. The man was incredibly stingy when it came to resources, but thoughtful when it came to knowledge, sending books and bodybuilding insights, even hiring a mentor to teach him mind-reading and other Neuromanifestation arts. He was also strict and devoted to the cloth, present mostly from afar. When he was around, he scolded Ged for even dreaming of an end to his Mind Shadow, drilling diligence and effort above all else.

Ged set his jaw. _It doesn’t matter. I’ll find him._ He knew his father to be an unfathomable figure; to him there was no one more qualified to speak about anything related to his Mind Shadow.

The tunnel opened onto a sprawling, ringed underground city, with glowing mud skyscrapers, streets lit by living lampposts, and animal vehicles roaming by, their crooked car doors etched into flesh.

He followed the main streets past merchant department stores, then turned down dim alleys lined with dilapidated mud houses, until he stepped into the light of a seldom-used side street.

Ged walked down the block to his destination.

A marble steeple rose before him, flawless white pillars holding aloft exquisitely carved stone. At the top stood the temple’s insignia: a peakless mountain.

Nostalgia tugged at him. He saw a dimple-cheeked girl twirling in a simple dress, her thoughtful eyes fixed on a younger Ged as he sat distracted on the Temple steps, staring off at anything but her. A heavy smile crept onto his lips as the memory lingered.

_I can’t wait to tell her. She'll be astonished to hear my Mind Shadow is—_

A thin thread of cigar smoke coiled into the vision, snapping it apart. A shiver crawled up his spine.

He whipped his head around, scanning the street.

_Strange…_ The street was bleak and empty. No one in sight. _What was that?_

Shaking off the unease, he pushed open the doors and stepped inside.

A small crowd lined up to pay their respects to a candlelit figure meditating on a cushioned mat.

The figure was imposing. A gray beard trailed past his stomach, hiding little of his rippling frame. Long white hair fell over his shoulders. His azure eyes flickered, shifting from ethereal calm to warlike fury.

Ged stood in the doorway. _Father. Do you even remember who I am?_
