“Unless you reach Mountain Establishment within a year, your death is certain.”

Ged’s chest tightened. His breath hitched.

He knew Muscle Condensation and its thirteen levels all too well. Reaching the Third had taken him eight relentless years, and he could not imagine the struggle of reaching the Thirteenth. Each level was exponentially harder than the last.

Yet compared to ascending to Mountain Establishment, those thirteen levels were nothing more than a cakewalk.

The resources and training required were unfathomable. Even if he gathered every stone, every scrap, every ounce of effort from the past eight years, it would amount to little more than a drop in the bucket. Scavenging his way forward as he had before was no longer possible.

And even if he defied fate and somehow ascended, the Planet Blue Eagle Ordinance Office would never approve it within a year. The bureaucracy for an Ascension Permit was notorious for dragging on for years, if it was approved at all. To ascend without a permit would draw the wrath of the world authority. Worse still, Planet Blue Eagle itself would intervene. That was courting death.

“I… I don’t want to die.” His voice cracked as he spoke.

The despair of the task gnawed at him. His skin crawled, as if thousands of insects were chewing through his flesh.

“Father… what am I supposed to do? Please, help me.”

Gore stroked his beard. “Child. Your life and death are still uncertain. The Mountain is climbed in steps, not leaps. Be patient. Do not let despair consume you. Focus first on reaching the Fourth Level of Muscle Condensation. Then continue onward.”

_Be patient?_

Ged swallowed hard.

“Climb in steps, not leaps?” His face twisted in disbelief, despair boiling into anger.

“Father, be serious. I’ve been taking steps my whole life. If I keep going like this, how can I possibly reach Mountain Establishment? I need to improve my routines. I need vast resources. I need to push permits through the authorities. How can all of that be achieved in a few simple steps?”

Gore sighed, his tone heavy. “You cling too tightly to outcomes. Gambling on shortcuts will only will hasten disaster. Strive with all your strength, but if the result does not come, do not be afraid to accept fate.”

Ged’s eyes burned. “I’m sorry, Father. But I cannot agree. I will never sit still while my life and death remain uncertain. I hope you can understand that much.”

“Then there is nothing more for me to say.” Gore’s expression was solemn.

Ged’s teeth clenched. His fists balled until the blood stopped flowing to his knuckles.

He glared at his father, fury blazing, but Gore’s calm blue eyes smothered it at once.

_Damn it._

“I can’t count on your help? Fine. I’ve relied on myself this long.”

He rose from the Mind Magnification Stone and strode to the exit.

Gore sighed and followed at a distance, but his presence only made Ged quicken his pace. 

Leaving the Temple Ged vanished into the dim street. Once he was far enough away, his eyes stung. He slipped into a shadowed alley, where no one would see him break down.

Gore stood at the Temple’s entrance, his expression calm and unyielding.

“Better this way,” he muttered, though his eyes lingered.

Then his eyes flickered from blue to red. A frown darkened his face.

“Are you planning to hide outside my Temple all day?” His voice was cold. “Get out here, you little rat.”

He grasped at the air. A thin strand of cigar smoke unraveled and whipped toward him. The smoke multiplied, swelling until it filled the street. From its coils, Wattkinz’s grinning figure was dragged into view.

Cigar smoke curled from his lips. He raised his hands in mock surrender.  
“Father. No need for anger. I was only passing through. A coincidence, nothing more.”

“Bullshit.” Gore snorted. “Greedy child. Don’t think I don’t see you tailing him. You covet your brother’s mind, waiting for the day his Shadow consumes him so you can refine his thoughts into a workout supplement for yourself.”

Wattkinz chuckled, unbothered. “You think so little of me. I’d never be so cruel to my little brother Ged. He’s mischievous and reclusive. I only worry about his well being. Without friends or family, he’ll wither and die. And when he does… better to be surrounded by those who love him than opportunistic vultures who do not.”

Gore’s brow furrowed. “Know your place, Wattkinz. I know you’ve plateaued at the sixth level and hunger for more, but push him too far and you’ll force my hand. Let matters run their course. Ambition will only lead you to ruin.”

“Thank you for your guidance, Father.” Wattkinz spoke sincerely, but his grin never faded. “I will keep your words close.”

He turned to leave. A spark of muscle energy lit his cigar. Each drag sent plumes curling outward, smoke swelling into clouds that swallowed his body whole. In moments, only the haze remained.

Gore lingered at the entrance, his eyes fixed on the smoke as it thinned and disappeared, leaving silence behind.

_Click._  
From somewhere unseen came the click of a pulled lampstring.

Gore’s eyes flickered back from red to blue, his expression tranquil.

Ged walked quickly through the empty Bowl streets, his eyes still raw from tears, his father’s words echoing in his mind.

“That bastard is the same as always,” he spat. “He could at least pretend to give a damn.”

Inside, the black hole that held his Mind Shadow vented white smoke like a factory chimney. Some of it seeped into his body, heat crawling through his veins, causing subtle changes to his body. He didn’t notice. His thoughts tangled, each step dragging heavier than the last.

_Strength and resources._ Everything came down to that. If he had more, he wouldn’t be in this state.

“The Abyssal Mouth. Even if there’s only a pebble left, I will seize it.”

His walk broke into a run.

He tore through the lantern-lit streets, pedestrians shrinking back as if from a madman. His wild gaze darted ahead, unfettered by their stares.

At last, the main road ended. Bowl itself gave way, the earth splitting into a yawning cavern. Ged strode inwards. 

The passage twisted before opening onto a wide train platform.

Though the Abyssal Mouth gaped as a vast pit on the surface, reaching it was anything but simple. Meteor barrages would have long since reduced the land to rubble, but the planet itself had raised natural barriers across the crust. These barriers shielded Bowl from the Celestial Offerings, but in doing so they also sealed off direct access to the Mouth.

Bowl was carved into the planet’s crust, an underground city of three descending layers. Beneath them lay a fourth, plunging into the mantle, where the only entrance to the Abyssal Mouth waited. 

The only safe way down was through public transportation.

And that was where Ged was headed.

He slowed, eyes scanning the platform. Bodybuilders worked out in tense silence as they waited for their ride.

Most strained through burpee-like drills—chests slamming against stone, bodies snapping upward in explosive rhythm.

But one group made his expression change.

They sprawled across their own territory, sun tattoos carved into their shaved skulls. From a glance alone, their workouts were nothing like the rest.

One sat on the ground, heels digging into stone, forcing fissures open and shut. Blood streaked his legs where muscle tore and healed with every strain. Another lowered himself in and out of a pit using only his arms, chains looped around his neck, each descent pulling his shoulders taut until the veins in his chest glowed faintly red. A third hauled his chin above a bar, teeth clenched, arms trembling as though they might snap under his own weight.

Their presence radiated violence, a pressure that drove every other bodybuilder to keep their distance.

Ged stared enviously as they worked out, fixated on the brutal tearing of their muscles. _They must be practicing a high-grade workout routine to achieve such incredible muscle growth._

When he practiced weighted push-ups with the tip of the mountain peak, he had used more than ten times the weight these men were lifting, yet he couldn’t even get one hundredth of their muscle growth.

Then his brow furrowed.

_These men are clearly a part of the Golden Suns Gang. They seldom use public transport. What are they doing here?_

A bad premonition crept over him.

He turned his gaze away and joined the others in simple bodyweight drills. He didn’t dare copy the gangsters’ routines. Blindly performing a workout without proper form was akin to suicide.

After a moment, a hiss rose from the underground tunnel. Two emerald eyes gleamed like headlights as a slithering white snake appeared.

Its insides were hollowed into ribcage-bone passenger cars, visible through grotesque windows of flesh.

Toothless mouths stitched onto its sides gaped open as doors, allowing Ged and the others to step inside.

The passengers were vicious-looking, tattooed bodybuilders. Beyond the Golden Suns, some bore cloud tattoos in place of eyebrows, while others had thunderbolts stitched across their necks. _Cloud Gang. Thunder Gang. Golden Suns. All three rulers of the underground… here?_

They split the car in thirds, glaring across the aisle, one word away from a deadly brawl.

Ged’s skin crawled. Cigarette smoke stung his nose. Dozens of gangsters glared his way, a few built like monsters nearly twice his size.

He didn’t dare meet their gazes, unwilling to be the spark that set off the explosion.

Many of the non-gang affiliated bodybuilders who had come to the platform took one look inside the train and turned back immediately, unwilling to step foot into that pressure.

_Something big is happening at the Abyssal Mouth._

Ged’s chest tightened. For a moment, he considered leaving as well, but his resolve held. If he wanted resources, there was no room for retreat.

He found an open flesh chair near the Golden Suns, sat down, and shut his eyes, forcing himself to ignore the suffocating atmosphere.

The snake train lurched forward.

There were ten stations in all, three carved into each layer of Bowl, with the last opening onto the Abyssal Mouth itself. Ged had boarded at the third station of the first layer. Now the train slid toward the fourth, the first of the middle tier, its arrival heralded by the blare of a flesh horn.

When the doors opened, Ged still had his eyes closed, lost in thought.  
_Is it a territory dispute? Or perhaps a treasure has surfaced at the Abyssal—_

“Sir.” A voice cut through his thoughts. He ignored it.

A tap landed on his shoulder. “Sir, excuse me.”

Ged opened his eyes. A young bodybuilder stood before him, broad-shouldered, devilish in appearance. No gang tattoos marked his skin. His smile was polite, without a trace of malice.

“What?”

“Could I have your seat?”

Ged glanced around. Dozens of seats sat empty.

“What’s wrong with those?”

“My back has been ailing me. I’ve ridden this train many times, and through trial and error I found that the seat you are sitting in works the best for my condition. If you don’t mind moving, I would very much appreciate it.”

The man’s tone was mild, almost apologetic. Ged’s irritation flared, but there was no rudeness to seize on, no excuse to snap back. The patience in the man’s eyes made refusal feel strangely difficult.

“Fine,” Ged muttered. He rose and crossed to the opposite side of the car. He closed his eyes again, forcing the man from his mind.

The train rolled onward. At the next stop, the voice returned. Softer.  
“Sir…”

Ged’s eyes snapped open. The devil-faced man loomed once more, smiling sheepishly.

“My apologies. That seat wasn’t quite right after all. But yours… could I try it, and see if it works better for me?”

“Are you looking to die?”

“No, no,” the man said quickly, hands raised in mock surrender. “Nothing like that. Just your seat is more than enough.”

Ged froze, glancing around. For the first time, he noticed something strange. Despite so many vicious gangsters glaring, none dared to look in the direction of the man in front of him. It was as if he were a plague. No one wanted to be involved.

Ged’s heart kicked in his chest.

The man’s smile never wavered. 
“You don’t mind, do you? After all it's nothing much… it’s only a seat.”
