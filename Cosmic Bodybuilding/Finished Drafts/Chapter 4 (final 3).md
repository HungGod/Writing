The congregation knelt in candle smoke. Ged watched from the entrance.

“Preceptor Gore,” one of the bowing men whispered, his voice brittle as glass. “I am but a climber lost upon the slopes of Mount Existence. I beg your guidance.”

“Speak, and I will listen.” Gore’s voice was soft, yet rolled like thunder through the hall.

“Last night I strayed… I sought the Peak of Existence.” His chest hitched; terror widened his eyes. “This morning I woke up with a demon in my mind.”

Gore stroked his beard. “What turned you from the path?”

“My growth has stumbled. My workouts once carried me to the first level, but now I’ve plateaued. I fear there’s nothing left for me in this life. Without growth, my climb has lost its purpose, and my thoughts tread only toward the end.”

Gore closed his eyes as he listened. When the man finished, he opened them to speak.

“When climbing, it is best to avoid thoughts of the end. To climb is to suffer. In suffering, it is easy to miss the path before you, with our minds drifting toward the bliss of the finish."

“But the end of one is the end of all. The Peak of Existence is the end of all existence."

“The climb must never be allowed to end.”

Gore's eyes burned. The man shivered.

“As for your demon… starve it of your thoughts; it will pass in time.”

The man lifted his head to meet Gore’s. “Thank you for your guidance, Preceptor. These days have been hard...” He reached into his abs and pulled out a nutritional stone, offering it with both hands. “Please, grant me a day’s quiet from my troubling mind.”

Gore stood and received the stone, looking deeply at the man.

“Today I grant you relief. Tomorrow is yours to endure,” Gore intoned. “Go, sit before me, and receive this blessing.”

The man moved, sitting cross-legged at his feet, facing him.

Gore clasped his hands in prayer. Neural essence crackled like lightning between his fingers; his body emanated resplendent white light. Muscle energy pulsed, rippling the air and lifting dust.

He spread his hands apart. Sparks leapt back and forth and wove threads of light. Between his palms, the outline of a worm took shape.

A ring of recognition burst outward, and the worm solidified into a corporeal form, visible to all.

Ged felt the ring pass through him, his worries easing as it crossed.

_Thought Burrowing Worm… no matter how many times I see Father perform the Temple’s secret Neuromanifestation techniques, it never fails to impress._

The worm writhed between Gore’s fingers. He lowered it to the man’s ear. It perked, as if sensing something, then burrowed eagerly into his skull.

The man’s face slackened with relief; his eyes like vacant saucers. Wordlessly, he rose, bowed once, and left toward the exit, brushing Ged’s shoulder as he passed.

Ged watched him go. Whatever the man had experienced in that moment was not for him to know; only a Temple Preceptor would be privy to such things.

Gore’s gaze found Ged for the first time since Ged had entered.

“I have recognized your arrival. What is it you need, child?”

“Father, I’ve come to seek your opinion on a personal matter. I hope you can find the time for me.”

Gore frowned, stroking his beard. He was silent for a long moment.

“Very well. Wait in the back chamber. I will see you when I am finished.” His eyes slid away, already fixed on the next patron stepping forward to bow.

Instinctively, Ged frowned. Dissatisfaction flickered, then faded. Without another word, he left the central platform and slipped into the curtained side chamber.

He stepped inside. Polished white skulls lined the walls like library shelves. A workbench crowded with beakers and vials held fluids and stones. At the room’s center stood a block of white marble; Ged knew it as his father’s Mind Magnification Stone. Its surface was alive with faint fluctuations of neural activity.

Ged sat on the Mind Magnification Stone and closed his eyes, curious about the newfound changes in his mind.

The stone vibrated with a low hum. Warmth spread through it and seeped into his skull.

His focus turned inward. His consciousness opened into a swamp of hair-thin reeds. Each reed was a connection, a past experience: the feel of grass, the smell of wet stone, the sight of fire. They multiplied without end. The reeds rose in small patches, their roots vanishing into the fog-gray swamp below. Ged pushed through the reeds, his feet sinking with every step as he neared his mind’s center.

There, suspended in midair near the center of his consciousness, was the pin-sized black hole that held his Mind Shadow. Orbiting nearby was the ocean sky fragment.

Ged first looked into the Mind Shadow's changes.

He tested it, feeding it agitation. The agitation floated in the air like a dark-spotted bubble. As it neared the black hole, it vanished.

“What is this?”

A thin puff of white smoke drifted out. It floated, then sank into the fog-gray swamp.

Ged grew curious. 

He summoned more agitation. Several more plumes of white smoke slipped into the swamp, but he gained no insight into what they were or where they went.

_Father said this swamp is filled with unparalleled danger and unfathomable depths. If one’s mental form goes in, it will lose all direction and never return. Where that white smoke is going, I can only guess._

Recognizing an impasse, he tried to peek inside the black hole: the resting place of his Mind Shadow.

“Is it asleep?” He found the black hole completely impenetrable; his senses couldn’t probe even a hair’s breadth inside. A suspicion formed in his heart.

He couldn't shake the feeling that this was not the end of his battles against his Mind Shadow.

But despite all his concerns, he at least confirmed that, for now, the Mind Shadow was harmless to him, which filled him with endless joy.

He next turned to study the incomplete ocean sky fragment.

With the effects of the Mind Magnification Stone he was able to study it more closely. As he peered deeper into the thumb-sized diamond shaped fragment, he found it to be incredibly vast. Beyond the blue surface of the fragment lay millions of blurry galaxies, each containing countless twinkling stars acting as lights. Since he only obtained 20% of the full fragment, he could only imagine what the complete fragment would look like.

Ged watched as the fragment orbited around the black hole. 

As it circled, he saw it was not uniform in its path.

"Is it being pulled into the black hole?”

A faint comet tail spiraled behind the fragment. Given enough time, the two would collide. For such an event to occur in his fragile consciousness, Ged could only imagine it wouldn’t be good.

Ged watched, worry pricking his features, and then something else caught his attention.

At one point in the fragment’s orbit, it jolted, and one of the four quadrants of the diamond glowed with chromatic light.

Ged narrowed his eyes, watching the fragment internally as it jolted.

The blurry galaxies in the flashing quadrant, projected an image that Ged could just make out.

"Is that Bowl?" He could make out the faintest signs of stone walls, encircling a black pit.

“But why? Is this because this fragment's origins come from the city gate? Or is this trying to tell me something more?”

Ged rubbed his chin in thought. But no answer surfaced.

With nothing more to test, he surfaced from his consciousness and opened his eyes in the Temple’s back room, still seated atop the Mind Magnification Stone.

With the new changes to his consciousness still lingering in his thoughts, he reached into his abs and drew out a clay tablet containing his expired permit.

_The untrained man at the gate was wrong; this permit was truly obtained through legitimate means, and I really am on my way to renew it._

Ged scanned the details. It was an Extraordinary Illness Permit, issued to those afflicted by a Mind Shadow or similar conditions that required excessive strength to keep at bay.

But as he continued to read, a wry expression formed. At the bottom, the permit required a five-stone renewal. _I am currently stoneless. Too poor to renew it._

His thoughts lingered on the Abyssal Mouth. His original reason for coming was to obtain resources, yet he had taken a long detour. A helpless expression crossed his face.

His thoughts snagged on his father’s slow-moving ways, and he sighed inwardly. He took out the empty vial of Mind Soothing pills and the fractured thug’s skull, studying each in turn.

_It was a steep cost, and even though the results are not yet clear, they seem promising._ He couldn’t help but smile at the empty vial. _At the very least I won’t need pills for the coming days._

He slid the vial back into his abs and cradled the fractured thug’s skull with both hands.

_I wonder what the origin of that ocean treasure truly is. Is it possible… can it be created a second time?_

Ged’s fingers traced the fractured skull, just about to utilize his mind reading technique, when the doorway curtain stirred. He froze.

“Father…” Ged watched as Gore emerged, his face unreadable.

“You are not going to believe what has happened… my Mind Shadow. It has lessened!”

Gore did not answer, not even with a nod. He inched closer.

“I feel free. Like an entire mountain was lifted off my back. For once in my life, I feel as if I have hope for recovery.”

Gore frowned.

The shift in his expression carved the joy from Ged’s words.

“Hold still.”

His fingers flared with muscle energy. He blurred forward and spread his hand across Ged’s head. His fingers sank deep into his scalp as he performed the mind-reading technique.

Without a Soul Taser, Ged felt quick, jolting pain. Yet Gore’s methods were far more advanced. His fingers traced each ridge with precise motions, combing over every inch of Ged’s scalp.

The pain, although unbearable, was gone quickly.

Gore withdrew his hands, leaving Ged slumped and gasping for air.

“Hmm…” Gore stroked his beard, eyes closed, furrows etching deep into his brow.

Ged finally propped himself up enough to meet his father’s eyes. _Could we not have had a simple conversation?_ Anger welled in his chest, but he said nothing.

Gore opened his eyes at last, his gaze slanted.

“I do not like it,” Gore murmured. “That ocean is no blessing. It reeks of demonic influence. It did not cure you—it hollowed out your mind. It treats the symptoms, not the cause. Your mind has become a garden for the Mind Shadow, forcing it to grow faster than ever before. Dormant, yes. But only for now. Soon it will return, stronger than ever.”

Ged swallowed. “Could you be mistaken?”

“I fear not.” Gore’s voice hardened.  
“Unless you reach Mountain Establishment within a year, your death is certain.”
