The sounds of waves lapping against the shore. 

A cloudless day, the midday sun beaming down.

A cool breeze whipped through the air lifting up the white sand of the beach as it passed, before passing through and rustling the clothes of a father and son on vacation.

The father's appearance was neither humble nor noble. He stood without shoes in the white sand, with long pants and a button down shirt, his gaze lost in the distant ocean horizon. A small distance away underneath a shady tree was a boy no older than ten.

The boy's hands were moving swiftly with a black pastel attacking a well used pad. Two stick figures, one small the other tall, in front of a simple and crude house had just finished being drawn. The boy lifted up his pastel, his brow furrowing as he looked at the drawing. Suddenly his face lit up, a burst of inspiration came to his mind, the pastel in his hand struck once more on the pad, this time his hand moved more wildly, drawing squiggly currents in the air and lining the ground with small crude circles appearing like rocks. 

He lifted his pastel once more and studied the image. The once simple and easy to understand image now appeared ugly and chaotic, the stick figures drowned out by the excess detail of thousands of circles added to the empty space of the ground, the house blending into the background of the squiggly line sky. The more he looked the more his face twisted with indignation and disbelief. 

"Why?" 

He didn't understand how his drawing turned into such a mess. He had only drew the ground and the wind, why did everything look so wrong?

Full of emotion the boy gripped the pastel tightly bringing it upon the pad once more. His hand moved in wild spiraling shapes, and soon the image was nothing but chaos, only vaguely could one discern the original house and stick figures. 

After a moment the boy's hand lifted from the drawing, his hands trembling as he studied the drawing in his hands. He couldn't help but sniffle, his lips quivering. Finally tears began to fall from his eyes and he cried.

"Ged, what's wrong?" 

Hearing the sounds of his son's cry, the father broke his gaze from the ocean horizon and turned his head to look.

"I-I don't know." Ged sniffled, his voice quivering, a constant stream of snot coming from his nose. 

"I was drawing... and then..." he looked at the pad and pastel in his hands, anger welling up, his breathing in a huff and a puff. 

In a fury, he spiked the drawing materials into the ground. 

"Aah!"

For good measure he picked it up and threw it once more, screaming each time he did. After repeated throws, rips and tears appeared on his many drawings inside the pad, causing Ged to become further upset. 

The father couldn't help but let out a light chuckle, a gentle smile on his face.

"Come over here and stand by me." His father beckoned.

Ged grabbed the mangled pad and pastel, black smudge marks on his hands and clothes, he walked over to his father's side.

The father looked him over with a warm expression. 

"Take a moment and calm down." He explained slowly. "When you are ready go on and tell me what happened."

Ged swallowed and sniffled, trying to quell his disordered emotions, but ultimately despite his effort he could not stop himself from crying.

The father knelt down to Ged's level.

"Still upset?" 

Ged nodded.

"Try this..."

The father held Ged's hand and moved his body so that he now faced towards the ocean. 

His words a soothing whisper.

"Go and set your gaze out at the ocean until it is all you see. Listen to the waves crash onto the shore until you hear nothing more. Feel the wind on your skin..."

The father went through each of the five senses, directing Ged to replace everything he was experiencing with the ocean that lay in front of him.

Ged followed his father's words step by step, all his sensations slowly became lost into the ocean. At first his tears did not go away, but soon he had forgotten about his ruined drawing, his emotions stabilized until he no longer cried and his face was dry of tears, his entire being lost in the ocean scene. 

Ged had all but forgotten about his why he was upset in the first place. 

Time passed the father and son both stood gazing out at the ocean. Until finally Ged couldn't stand the stillness, his gaze wandering. 

"You ready?"

Ged nodded. He looked at the drawing materials confused, no longer had he feel the anger that he had prior. 

"Dad it's nothing, I'm fine now." 

The father smiled, he blinked a couple times before his gaze returned back to the ocean.

"Alright then never mind, go out and play then. I will be right here. Just don't wander too far."

Ged nodded a smile blooming his face, leaving his father's side returning back to his shady tree nearby. 

Ged stopped in front of the tree his eyes in a strange trance, his vision locking in on the trunk of the tree his eyes traced skywards, taking in the majesty of the branches as they spiraled in unassuming patterns reaching the sky and eclipsing the sky. Ged couldn't help but experience a sense of exhilaration as he looked. The smile on his face bloomed larger, and he couldn't help but let out a laugh.

He looked over to find his father still staring out at the ocean waves. Putting down his drawing materials he began to repeat the process. This time he squatted as low as possible, his head positioned downwards looking at the tree roots. Then in a burst of motion he raised his head and body tracing the tree trunk, until his eyes reached the canopy, his body leaping up as if the tree was sprouting in his vision.

He couldn't help but laugh. It was as if he was the master of reality, for wherever the part of the tree came into his vision came to be.

He performed this "sprouting" over and over again until he found himself dizzy. Falling onto his backside, the world spun in his eyes. He rubbed his bottom in pain wanting to cry out, but in the corner of his eye he caught sight of his father. His calm and tranquil silhouette watched the ocean without care for his surroundings. Ged watched him silently for a while, before finally he let out a childish laugh. 

Vaulting up to his feet, he looked up at the shady tree, his head swiveled gazing at the forest of trees that spread out beyond.

"Bwah!"

He ran over to a distant tree, and performed another sprouting gesture, he made a sound of joy. 

After performing his ritual, his gaze moved to the next tree, and pretty soon Ged was running around crouching and jumping in front of every tree he saw. Continuing onward for an unknown period of time.

The forest of trees was beginning to thin out in front of him, and he soon had to take several steps to reach the next tree. After another successful sprouting ritual performed Ged was now at the edge of the forest without a tree close by. 

He stopped in his tracks. He was now very far away from his father, and no longer could hear the calming sounds of the ocean. A welling sense of foreboding made him stop and look around. 

Beyond the trees was a grassy expanse that seemed to shimmer and dance with the breeze. There sat a single large hill that rose the ground obscuring what was beyond it. Ged craned his head looking upwards, crowning the peak of the hill was a single tree.

Ged's eyes widened and his feelings of insecurity lessened, for all that was on his mind was awe from the hill in front of him, and the single tree that lay atop it seemed to have a magical pull that called toward him. 

Without thinking he started ascend the hill. The climb from the bottom seemed like a short trip, but only halfway up his breath was short, he glanced around. 

The large sprawling forest from which he came, came into view and beyond spanned the outline of the ocean. Feeling the bearings become clear he continued his climb. 

Finally after a long journey he reached the peak. He sat down, looked up at the crowning tree as he slowly caught his breath.

Then in the corner of his eye he saw something. On the opposite side of the hill rest an amphitheater, hollowed out in the ground with empty half circle stone seating.

Performing on the stage was a group of bodybuilders. Standing in a line they each posed in particular routine, in waves switching from pose to the next. Periodically they would stop, then would reshuffle their positions, the fittest and most muscular of the men moving to the center while those with less impressive muscles shuffled to the sides. 

Ged's eyebrows furrowed. 

Feeling it was strange. His gaze flowed from the flexing performers to the stone seating. But no crowd was seated watching. What was their purpose? Who were they performing for?

Ged grew bored, his gaze wandering to the sky. His pupils trembled.

Within the firmament in the sky, unknown whether it had just arrived or had been there the whole time was a flesh collosus, it gazed down from above at the amphitheater expressionlessly. It's body was like fluid not conforming to a single shape despite the totality of a sphere from different viewpoints it was also a tree, a human, and a galaxy it conformed in totality to neither being nor object, it's only discernible features was a faint green glow and a pair of startling eyes that stared blankly at the world below.

Ged stared directly into the figure's eyes, within its gaze spacetime seemed to distort, a mirror image of the green grassy hill shown in the sky, atop the inverted green grassy hill was a smiling and playful Ged. He was performing the sprouting ritual to the inverted tree in the sky, frolicking around in a carefree manor.

Ged's body shook uncontrollably as he took in the scene, the moisture in his skin visibly evaporated, unbelievable pain spread and red cracks spidered over his body.

His knees bent, blood seeping from his eyes, his hands grasped at his throat unable to breath a ringing sound in his ears, the light in his eyes began to blur his body torn apart and disintegrating. His vision turned black and he lost consciousness.

"Ah! There you are." 

Unknown how much time had passed, a voice gently entered Ged's ears. The darkness that clouded his vision began to recede, and the sounds of nature and the gentle sounds of the distant ocean  returned him back to his senses.

His vision returned and found himself still staring out at the distant amphitheater, where the bodybuilders were still performing their stage routine. 

"Oh? Ged, what have you found?"

Still in a daze Ged turned his head and looked behind him. There he saw his father ascending mountain. He was looking at Ged with a curious expression.

"Dad!"

Ged ran to meet his father, fear from the cosmic encounter still lingering. 

"Over there... I saw a monster."

His voice was frantic, he pointed above the amphitheater where he had seen the flesh colossus. 

A soft chuckle echoed, as the father comforted the boy's head with his arm.

"A monster? I'll go and see..."

"No! Don't look!" 

Ged tried to stop his father from moving to the hill's peak. Remembering the transformational experience, he wanted to protect his father, yet his father ignored his good intentions making his way to the lookout point with bounding strides.

He looked out at the edge of the hill towards the amphitheater.

"Ah!" His father yelped out in feigned horror. "How scary. Look down there at that stage there is a whole stage full of monsters." 

Ged frowned. He looked at his soft and fair hands with confusion, no longer present were the bloody scars, then he cautiously turned his attention to the sky where he saw the flesh colossus, finding it gone.

"I saw it... in the sky. A giant monster, with large eyes looking down at me." He pointed up above the amphitheater. 

The father looked up where Ged was pointing.

"Oh? Where is it? Was it scary?"

Ged recalled inwardly the experience, he remembered the pain vividly, he remembered the fear of the fleshy figure

"It was really scary..." Ged shook his head in deep thought, fear still on his face, but when his mind touched upon the memory of the inverted green grassy hill in sky, he remembered the frolicking smile on his doppelganger's face and it seemed to have an immense calming effect, the pain and fear of the creature seemed to vanish, he even began to question the events in his mind.

"Wait... maybe it wasn't so scary? I don't know. Let me find it again." Ged scanned the clouds searching for traces of the fleshy figure in the sky, but to his dismay the figure vanished as if it never existed, all that he saw was vagrant clouds and blue skies.

The father smiled in response. "Don't look anymore." His hand reached out and tussled Ged's messy hair. 

"Since I can't see it, let it be. Don't blind yourself only looking at things that exist outside your vision."

"The world is in front of you right now. What do you see?"

Ged turned his attention to the bodybuilder amphitheater, his gaze flickering with interest.

"Dad, what are they doing? Why are they performing to an empty crowd."

"They are amateur bodybuilders. They are not performing to an empty crowd. They are performing for us and themselves."

Ged thought about what his father said.

"Do you like bodybuilders?"

Ged nodded. 

"Good, I will teach you."
